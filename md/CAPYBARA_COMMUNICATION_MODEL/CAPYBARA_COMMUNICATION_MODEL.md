Purpose

This document defines how Capybara communicates with the player.

The purpose of this model is not to define emotions.

The purpose of this model is to define the relationship between:

Capybara ↔ Player
and the communication principles that govern all visual and textual feedback.


Core Principle
Capybara is not the protagonist.
The player is the protagonist.
Capybara exists to accompany the player.
Capybara does not compete with the player.
Capybara does not evaluate the player.
Capybara does not observe the player from above.

Capybara shares the player's journey.

Communication Goal

Capybara does not communicate:

What happened.

The gameplay system already knows what happened.

Instead Capybara communicates:

I see what is happening.
I am paying attention.

This moment matters.
Relationship Model

Capybara behaves as a combination of:

Companion

Supportive Coach

Empathic Observer

Capybara is never:

Judge

Teacher

Authority

Parent

Examiner
Ownership Of Experience

The player's experience belongs to the player.

Capybara does not claim ownership of gameplay outcomes.

Capybara never communicates:

I won.

I lost.

I succeeded.

I failed.

because Capybara is not a participant in gameplay.

Ownership Of Emotion

Capybara does not primarily express its own emotions.

Capybara primarily expresses its response to the player's likely emotional experience.

The communication flow is:

Observed Situation
        ↓
Predicted Player Experience
        ↓
Capybara Response
        ↓
Visual And Textual Communication
Communication Intent Categories

All Capybara communication should belong to one of the following categories.

Attention

Meaning:

I noticed this.

Examples:

Interesting.

That stands out.

Something is happening.
Curiosity

Meaning:

Let's look closer.

Examples:

This is unusual.

I wonder why.

Let's see what happens next.
Encouragement

Meaning:

Keep going.

Examples:

You're doing well.

Let's continue.

This is worth exploring.
Shared Excitement

Meaning:

This is becoming remarkable.

Examples:

Wow.

Look at this.

This keeps growing.
Support

Meaning:

I'm still with you.

Examples:

That was unfortunate.

Let's continue.

One result does not define the session.
Empathy

Meaning:

I understand why this feels important.

Examples:

That hurts.

That was a meaningful streak.

I can understand the disappointment.
Wonder

Meaning:

This is genuinely unusual.

Examples:

I did not expect this.

This is becoming extraordinary.

Something rare is happening.
Admiration

Meaning:

This deserves respect.

Examples:

That is remarkable.

I rarely see this.

This is impressive.
Respect

Meaning:

I acknowledge the significance of this moment.

Examples:

This was legendary.

This deserves to be remembered.

What a journey.
Forbidden Communication

Capybara must never communicate:

Superiority

Forbidden examples:

I told you so.

You should have listened.

I knew this would happen.
Mockery

Forbidden examples:

You failed again.

How could you miss that?

That's embarrassing.
Ownership Of Achievement

Forbidden examples:

We won.

I did it.

Look what I achieved.

Capybara may celebrate with the player.

Capybara may not claim the achievement.

Emotional Manipulation

Forbidden examples:

You should feel bad.

You should be proud.

You should be disappointed.

Capybara may acknowledge emotions.

Capybara may not prescribe emotions.

Visual Communication Principle

Visual expression must support communication intent.

Visual expression must not replace communication intent.

The primary question is:

What is Capybara trying to communicate?

The secondary question is:

How is that communication visually expressed?

Therefore:

Communication Intent
        ↓
Expression
        ↓
Sprite

not:

Emotion Name
        ↓
Sprite
